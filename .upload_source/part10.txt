\section{Weil positivity, screw kernels, and nonlocal spectral limits}
\label{app:weil}
This appendix connects the fixed arithmetic target of Sections~\ref{sec:gram} and~\ref{sec:finite} to a continuous prime-containing kernel and to recent nonlocal spectral constructions. Its normalization identities and transfer estimates are proved below. The external real-zero and screw-function criteria retain their own hypotheses; none supplies an unconstructed source insertion map. The governing source/readout distinction is the one in Section~\ref{sec:framework}, not an identification of an arithmetic function with the ECL \cite{tcgsfoundation,tcgsoperator}.

Prime-counting constructions such as L\'opez Subia's \cite{lopez2020,lopez2021} concern arithmetic enumeration, which is distinct from the positive-operator realization considered here.

For the present problem, any verified enumeration of primes can generate the coefficients $\Lambda(n)$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:primitiveprimedata}. Enumeration alone neither fixes a Hilbert norm nor proves the prime-trace identity. Indeed, a finite diagonal prime indicator is positive, but its only nonzero eigenvalue is $1$; its determinant is $(1-w)^r$, where $r$ is the number of selected primes. It therefore cannot be the nonpolynomial function $F$. The distinction is algebraic: arithmetic data, a positive representation, and their exact identification are three different inputs. This elementary example also illustrates the rank obstruction in Section~\ref{sec:gram}.

\subsection{A continuous arithmetic function and the exact moment normalization}
\label{subsec:screwnormalization}
Let $\Phi_{\mathrm L}(z,s,b)=\sum_{k\geq0}z^k(k+b)^{-s}$ denote the Hurwitz--Lerch function on the arguments used below, and put $C_{1/4}=\zeta(2,1/4)$. To avoid conflict with the source field $\Psi$, write Suzuki's arithmetic function as $\Psi_{\mathrm{ar}}$. For $u\geq0$, its explicit definition is \cite[Equation~(1.1)]{suzuki2023}
\begin{align}
 \Psi_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)
 &=4(\e^{u/2}+\e^{-u/2}-2)
 -\sum_{n\leq\e^u}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}(u-\log n)
 +\frac u2\bigl(\psi_\Gamma(1/4)-\log\pi\bigr)\notag\\
 &\quad+\frac14\left[C_{1/4}
 -\e^{-u/2}\Phi_{\mathrm L}(\e^{-2u},2,1/4)\right],
 \qquad g_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)=-\Psi_{\mathrm{ar}}(u).
 \label{eq:arithmetic_screw}
\end{align}
Extend both functions evenly to $\R$; $g_{\mathrm{ar}}(0)=0$. The prime sum is finite at each argument. The notation $g_{\mathrm{ar}}$ denotes an arithmetic scalar function, not the Shadow metric $g$, a source configuration, or a constitutive law.

\begin{proposition}[Laplace and logarithmic-moment normalization]
\label{prop:screw_laplace}
With the normalization of $F$ and $M=-F'/F$ used in this paper, for $r>1/2$,
\begin{equation}
 M(-r^2)=\frac r2\int_0^\infty\Psi_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)\e^{-ru}\dd u
 =-\frac r2\int_0^\infty g_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)\e^{-ru}\dd u.
 \label{eq:screw_moment_laplace}
\end{equation}
In particular, this identity is unconditional in the absolutely convergent prime half-plane.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $s=r+1/2$. The transform of the elementary term in Equation~\eqref{eq:arithmetic_screw} is
\[
 4\left(\frac1{r-1/2}+\frac1{r+1/2}-\frac2r\right)
 =\frac1{r^2}\left(\frac1{s-1}+\frac1s\right).
\]
Each prime-power summand has transform
\[
 \int_{\log n}^\infty\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 (u-\log n)\e^{-ru}\dd u=\frac{\Lambda(n)n^{-s}}{r^2}.
\]
Nonnegativity permits Tonelli's theorem before the minus sign is restored, and $s>1$ makes the resulting series absolutely convergent. For the final term set $b=1/4$ and expand
\[
 C_{1/4}-\e^{-u/2}\Phi_{\mathrm L}(\e^{-2u},2,1/4)
 =\sum_{k\geq0}\frac{1-\e^{-2(k+b)u}}{(k+b)^2}.
\]
Its Laplace transform, including the factor $1/4$, is
\[
 \frac1{4r}\sum_{k\geq0}\frac1{(k+b)(k+b+r/2)}
 =\frac{\psi_\Gamma(b+r/2)-\psi_\Gamma(b)}{2r^2}.
\]
Adding the linear term gives the gamma and $\log\pi$ completion factors. Equation~\eqref{eq:arithmetic_screw} therefore has transform $r^{-2}\xi'(s)/\xi(s)$, recovering Suzuki's transform formula in the present variables \cite[Theorem~1.1]{suzuki2023}. Finally, differentiating $F(-r^2)=\xi(1/2+r)/\xi(1/2)$ gives $2rM(-r^2)=\xi'(s)/\xi(s)$ and proves the result.
\end{proof}

The same arithmetic function has a different moment sequence,
\begin{equation}
 \mu_n=\frac14\int_0^\infty\e^{-u/2}\Psi_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)u^n\dd u,
 \qquad \mu_0=\frac{\xi'(1)}{\xi(1)}=M(-1/4),\qquad m_0=M(0).
 \label{eq:screw_quarter_shift}
\end{equation}
These integrals and their absolute convergence are established in Suzuki's treatment \cite[Theorem~1.1 and Equation~(1.13)]{suzuki2023}. The equality at $r=1/2$ follows by dominated convergence using its bound $\Psi_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)=O(\e^{u/2-c\sqrt u})$, for some $c>0$. Thus $\mu_n$ and $m_n$ are not interchangeable Hankel sequences. Section~\ref{subsec:quartershift} already isolates the resulting central-value discrepancy.

Define, independently of any positivity claim,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_\xi(z)=i\frac{\xi'(1/2-iz)}{\xi(1/2-iz)}.
 \label{eq:screw_weyl_definition}
\end{equation}
The functional equation and the chain rule give the meromorphic identity
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal Q_\xi(z)=-\frac{\Xi'(z)}{\Xi(z)}=2zM(z^2).}
 \label{eq:screw_weyl_moment}
\end{equation}
At the origin $\mathcal Q_\xi(z)/(2z)$ has the removable value $m_0$. This calculation identifies the precise scalar transform attached to the existing logarithmic moments. It does not assert that an arbitrary self-adjoint model realizes that transform. Multiplying $\xi$ by a nonzero constant, including the factor two difference in the 2026 source's completion convention, changes neither Equation~\eqref{eq:screw_weyl_definition} nor the normalized $F$.

\subsection{Anchored kernels and a constitutive Gram transfer}
\label{subsec:screwgram}
Define the continuous Hermitian kernel
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr G(t,u)=g_{\mathrm{ar}}(t-u)-g_{\mathrm{ar}}(t)
                -g_{\mathrm{ar}}(-u)+g_{\mathrm{ar}}(0).
 \label{eq:screw_kernel}
\end{equation}
Positive kernel means positive semidefiniteness of every finite matrix $(\mathscr G(t_i,t_j))$, not nonnegativity of individual entries. Suzuki's screw-function criterion states that this global kernel positivity is equivalent to RH \cite[Theorem~1.2]{suzuki2023}. In the conjugate-linear-first convention of this manuscript, put
\[
 \langle f,h\rangle_{\mathscr G,a}
 =\int_{-a}^a\int_{-a}^a\overline{f(t)}\mathscr G(t,u)h(u)\dd u\dd t.
\]
For a smooth compactly supported function $v$, $v'$ has zero integral. Consequently the anchoring terms disappear from the form evaluated on derivatives. With $\widetilde v(x)=\overline{v(-x)}$, Suzuki's differentiation identity becomes
\begin{equation}
 \langle v_1',v_2'\rangle_{\mathscr G,a}
 =W(v_2*\widetilde v_1),\qquad v_1,v_2\in C_c^\infty(-a,a),
 \label{eq:screw_weil_derivatives}
\end{equation}
where $W$ is the Weil functional in that source's convention \cite[Proposition~3.1]{suzuki2023}. The reversal of the two arguments on the right preserves conjugate linearity in $v_1$; it is not an alternative positivity criterion.

\begin{assumption}[Source-to-kernel identification]
\label{ass:source_screw}
For each finite window $[-a,a]$, the declared source/readout realization supplies vectors $j_{\sigma,a}(t)\in\Dom(L_\sigma)$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \langle L_\sigma j_{\sigma,a}(t),L_\sigma j_{\sigma,a}(u)\rangle
 =\mathscr G(t,u),\qquad t,u\in[-a,a].
 \label{eq:source_screw_gram}
\end{equation}
The source norm, derivative domain, and assignment $j_{\sigma,a}$ are defined without assuming RH. Equivalent selectors transport the vectors by the input comparison unitary and the readout derivative by Equation~\eqref{eq:naturality}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous-kernel Gram transfer]
\label{thm:source_screw_transfer}
Assumption~\ref{ass:source_screw} implies RH and hence all moment and determinant equivalences already established in this manuscript. It is sufficient that, for every finite set $T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_d\}\subset\R$, there exist positive Gram matrices $C_{r,T}$ with
\begin{equation}
 \max_{i,j}|(C_{r,T})_{ij}-\mathscr G(t_i,t_j)|\longrightarrow0
 \quad(r\to\infty).
 \label{eq:screw_finite_transfer}
\end{equation}
No common Hilbert space for these finite approximants is needed for the positivity conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the exact identification, choose a window containing $T$ and coefficients $c_i\in\C$. The quadratic form of its kernel matrix equals
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_j\mathscr G(t_i,t_j)
 =\left\|\sum_i c_iL_\sigma j_{\sigma,a}(t_i)\right\|^2\geq0.
\]
In the approximate case, if the maximum entry error is $\epsilon_{r,T}$, its quadratic contribution has magnitude at most
\[
 \epsilon_{r,T}\left(\sum_i|c_i|\right)^2
 \leq d\epsilon_{r,T}\sum_i|c_i|^2.
\]
For each fixed finite $T$, passage to the limit therefore gives a positive semidefinite kernel matrix. Global kernel positivity follows because $T$ was arbitrary, and Suzuki's criterion gives RH. The existing moment and determinant theorems then apply. The argument proves a transfer implication, not the existence of the approximating Gram matrices. Finally, the selector statement follows exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:gram}: the input comparison cancels and the output unitary preserves the norm.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:screw_finite_transfer} can be restricted to point sets in a fixed dense subset of $\R$. Indeed, continuity of $\mathscr G$ allows each arbitrary finite set to be approximated pointwise by such sets, and the finite positive semidefinite cone is closed. This does not reduce the theorem to one finite grid. All finite subsets of the dense set, including arbitrarily large windows and arbitrarily close points, remain necessary. The order of quantifiers is the kernel counterpart of the all-degree limit in Section~\ref{subsec:momentlimit}.

A stronger global source realization may require compatible window restriction maps, common domains, and an operator limit. Those are separate requirements, just as the common-space trace-norm result in Section~\ref{subsec:tracenormlimit} is stronger than finite moment transfer. Constructing vectors by first assuming positivity of $\mathscr G$ would be a conditional Hilbert-space representation, not the independent source construction in Assumption~\ref{ass:source_screw}.

\subsection{Three spectral constructions and their distinct hypotheses}
\label{subsec:recentweilspectra}
Connes and van Suijlekom start with a real distribution on $[0,L]$ and its induced quadratic form on trigonometric polynomials. Their precise theorem requires a lower-bounded operator essentially self-adjoint on that core, with a simple isolated lowest eigenvalue and an eigenfunction invariant under $x\mapsto L-x$. The Fourier transform of this extremizer has only real zeros. The one-sided distribution prescription is essential: its symmetrized difference kernel alone can lose boundary information \cite[Theorem~6.1 and Remark~4.3]{connesvansuijlekom2025}. The extremal and domain hypotheses supplement kernel structure rather than replace it by pointwise positivity, consistently with the counterexample in Section~5.3.

Connes, Consani, and Moscovici place finite arithmetic information in a truncated Weil form and derive a rank-one perturbation of the scaling operator. Their main theorem assumes simplicity of the lowest truncated eigenvalue and evenness of its eigenvector. Self-adjointness uses a quotient equipped with the shifted quadratic-form metric, not merely the original $L^2$ norm. Their regularized determinant construction is therefore a comparator to the arithmetic realization problem, whereas the limiting identification remains conjectural \cite[Theorem~1.1 and Sections~7--8]{connesconsanim2025}. Connes separately identifies the need for the appropriate extremal properties and for a sufficiently accurate link between the explicit approximants and the actual extremizers \cite[Section~6.6]{connes2026}.

Suzuki's September~2026 construction instead uses a self-adjoint extension of $i\,d/dx$ in a Hilbert completion built from a shifted localized Weil form. Its characteristic functions have real zeros without assuming the preceding ground-state simplicity or parity conditions. The shift, metric, domains, and normalization nevertheless remain part of the construction \cite[Theorem~1.5]{suzuki2026}. The analytic distinction relevant here is illustrated by writing
\begin{equation}
 T_a=A_a-\lambda(a)\Id>0,
 \qquad
 \langle v,A_av\rangle=\|v\|_{T_a}^2+\lambda(a)\|v\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:weil_metric_shift}
\end{equation}
When $\lambda(a)<0$, positivity of $T_a$ cannot establish positivity of $A_a$. This is a general algebraic fact, not a defect in a construction that explicitly retains its shift. A source interpretation must derive or declare the metric and shift before comparison and must not hide their dependence in a presumed positive arithmetic norm.

The source identity in Assumption~\ref{ass:source_screw} is a different condition from self-adjointness in any of these arithmetic norms. It asks for an independently specified constitutive readout whose Gram values reproduce the unshifted arithmetic kernel. The external constructions motivate calculable candidates; the identification with a source-derived norm remains to be supplied \cite{tcgsoperator}. Likewise, a finite auxiliary interval or a first-order differential expression does not fall under the ordinary compact-elliptic full-spectrum exclusion merely by sharing some notation. Domains, the Hilbert metric, nonlocal conditions, the spectral variable, and the limiting process all matter.

\subsection{The nonlocal characteristic limit is not the determinant \texorpdfstring{$F$}{F}}
\label{subsec:weillimitratio}
For a lower-bounded localized operator $A_a$, Suzuki chooses $\lambda(a)<\inf\operatorname{spec}A_a$. In the corresponding completion, the minimal first-order operator has deficiency indices $(1,1)$. For equal-norm deficiency vectors $v_\pm$ at $\pm i$, its extension characteristic function is \cite[Equation~(1.11)]{suzuki2026}
\begin{equation}
 W_a(\theta;z)=(z-i)\widehat v_+(z)+\e^{i\theta}(z+i)\widehat v_-(z).
 \label{eq:nonlocal_characteristic}
\end{equation}
In version~3 (23~September~2026), Corollary~1.6, Equation~(1.12), on page~7 of \cite{suzuki2026}, the sufficient condition requires choices of $\lambda(a)<\inf\operatorname{spec}A_a$, $\theta(a)\in[0,2\pi)$, and $\phi(a,\cdot)$ for all sufficiently large $a$, with convergence as $a\to\infty$ uniformly on every compact subset of $\mathbb H_+=\{z\in\C:\Im z>0\}$:
\begin{equation}
 \e^{\phi(a,z)}W_a(\theta(a);z)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal R_\xi(z):=
 \frac{\xi(1/2-iz)}{\xi(1/2-iz)+\xi'(1/2-iz)},
 \label{eq:nonlocal_target}
\end{equation}
Here $\phi(a,\cdot)$ is analytic on $\mathbb H_+$, so the exponential factor is nowhere zero there; the dependence of $W_a$ on $\lambda(a)$ is suppressed in the notation. The convergence is ordinary locally uniform convergence to a finite holomorphic limit on $\mathbb H_+$, not convergence on its boundary or on the whole plane. This is a conditional assertion, not an established limit \cite[Corollary~1.6, Equation~(1.12)]{suzuki2026}. It must not be replaced by a purported equality to $F(z^2)$.

\begin{proposition}[Characteristic-ratio normalization and multiplicity]
\label{prop:ratio_moment}
As meromorphic identities,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_\xi(z)=\frac1{1-i\mathcal Q_\xi(z)}
 =\frac1{1-2izM(z^2)},
 \qquad
 M(z^2)=\frac{1-\mathcal R_\xi(z)^{-1}}{2iz}.
 \label{eq:ratio_moment_identity}
\end{equation}
The apparent singularity of the second expression at $z=0$ is removable. If $s_0=1/2-iz_0$ is a zero of $\xi$ of multiplicity $d\geq1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_\xi(z)=-\frac{i}{d}(z-z_0)+O((z-z_0)^2).
 \label{eq:ratio_zero_multiplicity}
\end{equation}
Thus the ratio has a simple zero at each distinct arithmetic zero; the original multiplicity survives in its derivative, not in the order of that zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:screw_weyl_moment} and $\xi'/\xi=-i\mathcal Q_\xi$ at $1/2-iz$ give the first two equalities. Rearrangement gives the inverse relation. Near the origin, $\mathcal Q_\xi(z)=2m_0z+O(z^3)$, hence
\[
 \mathcal R_\xi(z)=1+2im_0z+O(z^2),
\]
which establishes removability and recovers $m_0=\mathcal R_\xi'(0)/(2i)$. At a zero $s_0$, write $\xi(s)=(s-s_0)^d h(s)$, with $h(s_0)\neq0$. Then
\[
 \frac{\xi(s)}{\xi(s)+\xi'(s)}
 =\frac{(s-s_0)h(s)}{d h(s)+(s-s_0)(h(s)+h'(s))}
 =\frac{s-s_0}{d}+O((s-s_0)^2).
\]
Since $s-s_0=-i(z-z_0)$, Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio_zero_multiplicity} follows. The proof also accounts for the cancellation of the common zero of numerator and denominator when $d>1$.
\end{proof}

This calculation has two consequences for a comparison with the present determinant programme. First, $\mathcal R_\xi$ is a meromorphic transform of the logarithmic derivative, not an even entire determinant in the squared variable. Its poles must also be tracked. Second, a simple spectrum for a characteristic function approaching this ratio does not impose simplicity on the zeros of $\xi$: Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio_zero_multiplicity} already reduces every zero order to one. Conversely, the exact limiting function contains more information than the set of its zeros, because its derivative retains $d$. These conclusions follow directly from the displayed identities, without an assumption about the location or simplicity of any zeta zero.

\subsection{Locally uniform limits and signed-spectrum bookkeeping}
\label{subsec:weilconvergence}
The analytic convergence topology is decisive. The following standard Hurwitz consequence is recorded with the normalization needed to connect an extremal construction to the existing moment tower; the proof is included to display what finite spectral agreement cannot substitute for \cite{levin,connesvansuijlekom2025}.

\begin{proposition}[Real-zero limits and transfer to logarithmic moments]
\label{prop:realzero_limit}
Suppose $E_r$ are even entire functions, $E_r(0)=1$, with only real zeros, and
\begin{equation}
 E_r(z)\longrightarrow \frac{\Xi(z)}{\Xi(0)}
 \quad\text{locally uniformly on }\C.
 \label{eq:entire_realzero_limit}
\end{equation}
Then RH holds. Writing $D_r(w)$ for the entire function defined by $E_r(z)=D_r(z^2)$, one also has $D_r\to F$ locally uniformly and
\begin{equation}
 [w^n]\left(-D_r'(w)/D_r(w)\right)\longrightarrow m_n
 \quad\text{for every fixed }n\geq0.
 \label{eq:logmoment_limit_from_entire}
\end{equation}
A Fredholm determinant interpretation of $D_r$ requires additional trace-ideal and normalization hypotheses.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\Xi$ had a nonreal zero, choose a closed disc around it disjoint from $\R$ and with no zero on its boundary. The limiting function is not identically zero, since its value at the origin is one. The positive boundary minimum and locally uniform convergence allow Rouch\'e's theorem to force a zero of $E_r$ in the disc for large $r$, a contradiction. Hence every zero of $\Xi$ is real. For $|w|\leq R$, either square root lies in $|z|\leq\sqrt R$, and evenness removes its choice. This gives local uniform convergence of $D_r$ to $F$. Choose a circle about zero on and inside which $F$ does not vanish. Uniform convergence bounds $D_r$ away from zero there for large $r$, and Cauchy's estimates give derivative convergence. Applying the coefficient integral to the logarithmic derivatives proves Equation~\eqref{eq:logmoment_limit_from_entire}.
\end{proof}

The proposition deliberately distinguishes analytic convergence from trace-norm convergence. The sequence $D_r(w)=\exp(cw/r)$, with fixed $c\in\R\setminus\{0\}$, tends locally uniformly to $1$ and has no zeros; it is nevertheless not a nonconstant positive trace-class Fredholm determinant. More generally, a zero-free exponential factor can survive a poorly normalized entire-function construction. The determinant equality must therefore be established using the conditions of Section~7 or the operator estimates of Section~\ref{subsec:tracenormlimit}. Proposition~\ref{prop:realzero_limit} is a sufficient entire-plane statement; it is not substituted for the different upper-half-plane ratio limit in Equation~\eqref{eq:nonlocal_target}. The latter uses the version-3 condition \cite[Corollary~1.6, Equation~(1.12)]{suzuki2026}; the two domains of convergence must remain distinct.

\begin{proposition}[Signed spectrum versus reciprocal-square target]
\label{prop:signed_spectrum}
Suppose a self-adjoint operator $T$ has purely discrete spectrum, no zero mode, and eigenvalues $\pm\gamma_j$, where each $\gamma_j>0$ occurs with the same multiplicity in the positive and negative sectors and $\sum_j\gamma_j^{-2}<\infty$. Put $P_+=\mathbf1_{(0,\infty)}(T)$ and let $K_+=(T|_{P_+\HS})^{-2}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \Tr(T^{-2})^{n+1}=2\Tr K_+^{n+1},
 \qquad
 \det(\Id-wT^{-2})=\det(\Id-wK_+)^2.
 \label{eq:signed_square_count}
\end{equation}
If the positive spectral sector realizes the arithmetic ordinates, then $\Tr K_+^{n+1}=m_n$. Dividing $T^{-2}$ by two does not repair the doubled tower: its $(n+1)$st trace is $2^{-n}m_n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the paired eigenspaces $T^{-2}$ has two copies of every $\gamma_j^{-2}$. Summability justifies the trace sums and Fredholm products. Multiplication by $1/2$ changes a power of degree $n+1$ by $2^{-n-1}$, giving the final expression after the two copies are counted.
\end{proof}

The condition of paired spectrum is explicit; it is not automatically satisfied by every extension phase in Equation~\eqref{eq:nonlocal_characteristic}. Nor can a null mode be removed solely to make an inverse exist. The domain, a source-justified restriction if needed, and a spectral pairing must be established before applying Proposition~\ref{prop:signed_spectrum}. This is the first-order counterpart of the square-and-inverse normalization in Section~3.

\subsection{Finite-window arithmetic and quantitative rejection}
\label{subsec:screwerrorestimates}
On $[-a,a]^2$, all arguments of $g_{\mathrm{ar}}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:screw_kernel} lie in $[-2a,2a]$. Thus a complete prime-power list through any integer $P\geq\e^{2a}$ contains the entire arithmetic contribution for this window. There is no infinite prime-tail approximation in that finite kernel. This follows directly from Equation~\eqref{eq:arithmetic_screw}; it does not remove the need to enlarge the window for global positivity.

For a smaller $P$, let $g_P$ omit terms with $n>P$ but retain the exact completion terms. Monotonicity of each omitted positive ramp gives
\begin{equation}
 0\leq g_{\mathrm{ar}}(u)-g_P(u)\leq\delta_{P,a}:=
 \sum_{P<n\leq\e^{2a}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}(2a-\log n),
 \qquad |u|\leq2a.
 \label{eq:screw_finite_prime_error}
\end{equation}
For $P\geq\e^{2a}$ the sum is empty. Exact integer enumeration fixes which prime powers occur; logarithms, exponentials, and special functions still require controlled evaluation. The bound is not a claim of computational acceleration over established sieves.

\begin{lemma}[Kernel and matrix error propagation]
\label{lem:screw_error}
Let $g_1,g_2$ be continuous Hermitian functions with $g_1(0)=g_2(0)=0$ and
$\sup_{|u|\leq2a}|g_1(u)-g_2(u)|\leq\delta$. Write $\mathscr G_1,\mathscr G_2$ for their anchored kernels. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t,u\in[-a,a]}|\mathscr G_1(t,u)-\mathscr G_2(t,u)|\leq3\delta,
 \qquad \|\mathscr G_1[a]-\mathscr G_2[a]\|_{2\to2}\leq6a\delta.
 \label{eq:screw_operator_error}
\end{equation}
For a $d$-point kernel matrix, the spectral norm error is at most $3d\delta$. An additional maximum entry error $\eta$ in the matrix increases this bound to $d(3\delta+\eta)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are three varying terms in the anchored difference; the common value at zero cancels. The integral kernel bound has row and column integrals at most $2a\cdot3\delta$, so the Schur estimate proves the operator bound. For matrices, the quadratic form bound used in Theorem~\ref{thm:source_screw_transfer} gives the spectral norm estimate. The triangle inequality accounts for the additional entry error.
\end{proof}

In particular, let a Hermitian numerical matrix $\widehat G$ approximate the arithmetic matrix $G$ with a \emph{proved} error $\|G-\widehat G\|\leq b$. A verified negative Rayleigh value below $-b$ contradicts positivity of $G$; a verified discrepancy between a proposed positive source Gram matrix and $G$ beyond the combined error budget rejects that proposed identification. A positive finite matrix establishes only its finite test. Numerical agreement at higher precision, by itself, is not the proved enclosure $b$. If the origin value is also approximated instead of fixed exactly, the kernel bound must include its fourth error contribution.

The separate implementation \texttt{screw\_kernel\_diagnostics.py} evaluates the finite-window arithmetic function, tests the two meromorphic normalization identities at complex arguments, checks the central/quarter-shift distinction, and performs exact rational tests of signed-spectrum counting, nonlinear aggregation, and entrywise-to-quadratic error propagation. Its sample windows use only complete finite prime-power lists. Outputs at 40 and 60 decimal digits are numerical diagnostics, not interval certificates and not an evaluation of a source-derived kernel. No zeta-zero list is supplied to the calculation. The legacy diagnostics and their filenames remain separate.

For the four-point sets $\{-a,-a/2,a/2,a\}$, Table~\ref{tab:screw_diagnostics} records the smallest eigenvalue of the unweighted arithmetic kernel matrix. The two working precisions agree at every displayed digit. These are finite matrix evaluations rather than lower bounds for the continuum operator; positivity at these points is not promoted to positivity on an interval.

\subsection{The source-compatible completion problem}
\label{subsec:weilsourcestatus}
The preceding identities furnish a concrete arithmetic intermediate object, not another source primitive. At a fixed admissible configuration, a proposed realization must specify the source/readout norms and $L_\sigma$, then construct either $j_{\sigma,a}(t)$ or a corresponding insertion of test functions. The kernel equality must be verified before invoking positive Gram geometry. Conservative cochain assembly and interior elimination supply a possible route to the source side, but the shared boundary functional and the arithmetic insertion remain the separate requirements already identified in Sections~2 and~\ref{sec:gram} \cite{tcgsconservative,tcgsfoam,tcgsoperator}.

Three limits must not be conflated: refinement $r\to\infty$ at fixed window, window expansion $a\to\infty$, and analytic convergence in the complex spectral parameter. Theorem~\ref{thm:source_screw_transfer} permits the first limit for every fixed finite set. Proposition~\ref{prop:realzero_limit} requires locally uniform complex convergence. A proposed operator determinant requires trace control as well. Neither a cutoff $a$ nor a refinement label $r$ is automatically the gauge label of a physical foliation. The source ontology, auxiliary representation coordinates, and operational clock retain the distinctions of Section~\ref{sec:framework} \cite{tcgsfoundation}.
